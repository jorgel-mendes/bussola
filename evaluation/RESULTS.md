# Privacy–utility sweep — results

**Run:** 7 ε × 5 cohort sizes × 200 trials = **7,000 simulated releases**, seed
`20260906`, 2h47m wall clock.
**Harness:** [`evaluation/sweep.py`](sweep.py) · **Raw data:** `evaluation/results/sweep.csv`
(regenerated by the `Privacy-utility sweep` workflow and attached to the run).

Every release runs the shipped mechanism through the shipped context builder and
the shipped epsilon split. Nothing here is a reimplementation — see the module
docstring for why that mattered enough to refactor the release path for it.

## 4. The SPEC's predicted headline is wrong

SPEC §8 set the target sentence for the demo:

> *"At ε = 1.0 with 25 plants, 96% of plants are assigned to the correct
> quartile."*

**Measured: 47.6%.** Not off at the margin — off by a factor of two, on the
sentence the demo was built around. It was a plausible guess written before
anything had been run, and running it is what this sweep is for.

The honest replacement, from the same table:

> **At ε = 2.0 with 100 contributors, 94% of contributors land in the correct
> quartile, and no release in 200 trials was unusable.**

## 5. The finding: ε·N ≈ 200 is the iso-utility contour

Take the cells that clear 90% correct **and** produce no unusable release in 200
trials. There are exactly three, and they are strikingly regular:

| ε | N | ε·N | correct | unusable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.0 | 25 | 200 | 91.7% | 0.0% |
| 4.0 | 50 | 200 | 94.0% | 0.0% |
| 2.0 | 100 | 200 | 94.4% | 0.0% |

**To halve the privacy cost, double the cohort.** That is the trade-off the
product exists to manage, stated as one number an operator can act on, and it is
measured rather than argued.

It also sets the price of the guarantee honestly: a consortium that wants ε ≤ 1
— the value OpenDP itself treats as typical — needs roughly 200 contributors for
quartiles at this quality. The Bahia demo consortium has 106.

## 6. Epsilon cannot buy its way out of a small cohort

The N = 5 column barely moves: 36.8% at ε = 0.1, and still only 50.8% at ε = 8 —
a privacy level so weak that **OpenDP prints a warning when it is used**. Eighty
times the privacy cost buys fourteen points, and the release is still wrong half
the time.

**And the floor flatters it.** At N = 5, ε = 0.1, all three true quartile points
land in a *single* noisy bucket 93% of the time: the release does not
misclassify some contributors, it collapses everyone into one quartile. The
released interquartile range has a median width of **2,791 MJ/t against a true
IQR of 427** — six and a half times too wide. A correct-quartile rate of 37%
there is not "37% useful"; it is the score you get for free by putting everyone
in the same bucket, because 2 of 5 contributors genuinely belong there.

This is why the unusable-rate table is reported beside the headline rather than
behind it. At N = 5, ε = 0.1 it reads 82%, and 82% is the truth.

**Consequence for the architecture.** Cohort size dominates epsilon across the
whole grid. That is the empirical case for two decisions previously argued from
theory: the minimum-contributor suppression threshold, and central DP over local
DP ([ADR-0002](../docs/adr/0002-central-dp-over-local-dp.md)) — under local DP
each contributor noises its own value, which is the small-N regime by
construction, at N = 1.

## 7. Corroboration of the Sprint 2 observation

Sprint 2 found a six-contributor cohort publishing q75 below q25 on the deployed
hub, and the demo preparation separately measured that beat firing **73% of the
time over 120 trials** at the demo's parameters.

This sweep, run independently on synthetic cohorts, gives **74.5% unusable at
N = 5, ε = 1.0**. Two different measurements, different code paths, same number.
The N = 6 result was not a fluke of one seeded dataset.

## 8. What this changes

1. **The demo's release parameters.** The seeded consortium is 50/50/6 and the
   demo releases at ε = 1.0, which the table puts at 66.9% correct for the
   N = 50 cohorts. The demo should either release at a higher ε or say the
   number out loud — showing a benchmark while knowing a third of members are
   misplaced is the failure mode this project exists to argue against.
2. **`S3-2` accuracy intervals** now have their source. The exponential
   mechanism returns no interval from `summarize()`; these per-cell error
   distributions are the simulation that replaces it.
3. **`S3-3` privacy–utility curve** reads §1 and §2 directly.
4. **The article's central figure** is §5 — the iso-utility contour — with §6 as
   the caveat that keeps it honest.

## 9. Limits of this measurement, stated

- **One generative process.** Lognormal plant means matching `datagen`. A
  different sector shape would move the numbers; the *ordering* of the effects
  is unlikely to change, but that is an assumption, not a result.
- **One metric's bounds.** The candidate grid spans 1,760–7,100 MJ/t. The
  exponential mechanism selects from that grid, so a metric with a wider
  declared range would do worse at the same ε — bounds are a utility parameter,
  not just a safety one.
- **200 trials per cell.** The unusable rate is a proportion over 200 draws, so
  one standard error is about 3.5 points near 50%. The correct-quartile rate is
  a mean of per-trial rates rather than a simple proportion, so its error is
  smaller but not analytically clean here. Either way: differences of a point or
  two between adjacent cells are not real, and the gradients across the table
  are. Two cells worth naming as noise rather than signal — N=5 at ε=0.5 (38.3%)
  reading above N=5 at ε=1.0 (36.0%), and the whole N=5 column's wobble.
- **ε = 8.0 is included as an upper anchor, not a recommendation.** OpenDP warns
  above 5. It is in the grid to show the ceiling, and §6 is what it shows.
