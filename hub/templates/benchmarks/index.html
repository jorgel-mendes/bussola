The interval comes from simulation, not from the mechanism. OpenDP’s summarize() returns no accuracy interval for the exponential mechanism used for quantiles — only the mechanism scale, shown above. So the band is measured instead: across 200 releases at this ε and cohort size on synthetic data with known ground truth, 9 in 10 landed within the error shown.

It brackets the true value, given the released one — which is why it is not centred on the figure in the second column. The measurement runs the other way round (error around a known truth), and inverting it is asymmetric: a release 20% out could have come from a true value 25% above it or 17% below. The band is then held inside the metric’s declared bounds, shown below — a submission outside them is rejected at ingest rather than clamped, so the true value cannot be there, and a band reaching past them would assert something already ruled out. Where the measured error reaches 100% the band widens to the whole declared range, and the column says that plainly rather than printing two numbers that look like a finding. The privacy–utility trade-off, measured

Every benchmark on this page is a trade: more privacy means more noise means a less reliable answer. This is that trade measured rather than asserted — 7,000 differentially private releases through this system's own mechanism, scored against known ground truth on synthetic cohorts.

Exactly three points clear 90% correct with no unusable release in 200 trials — ε=8 at N=25, ε=4 at N=50, and ε=2 at N=100. Each multiplies to 200: to halve the privacy cost, double the cohort. Cohort size dominates epsilon everywhere on this chart. At N=5 the line barely rises at all — eighty times the privacy budget buys fourteen points, because at that size the release collapses every contributor into one quartile rather than misplacing a few.
